What the pipeline does, step by step

Can what's said in class tell when I'll raise my hand?

Every few seconds of a recorded class, the model reads what was just said, where the class is, and what I prepared beforehand, and guesses whether my hand goes up soon. My actual hand raises come from the recording: the yellow-green icon on my tile. The question is whether the words help, beyond simply knowing when in class it is.

What the model is allowed to see

At each moment t, a fixed-size snapshot of the past

The model never reads the whole transcript. Every 2 seconds it gets the same three blocks, all built from things that had already happened by t. Step 5 builds them, and a test deletes everything after t and checks the snapshot doesn't change.

① What was just said

The last 60 seconds of words, tagged by speaker role (professor, classmate, me). Names are masked and punctuation removed.

  • did the professor just ask a question?
  • how long has it been quiet?
② Where the class is

A handful of numbers:

  • minute of class, current activity (discussion, poll, lecture)
  • my raises so far this class, time since my last one
  • my usual rate in this course, from earlier classes only
③ What I prepared

The same all class: the pre-class questions, my workbook answers, the session's learning outcomes.

  • how close is what's being said right now to what I wrote?
Never allowed

Anything after t: the professor saying "Brady, go ahead", my own speech after the raise, the class's total raises, a lesson segment that hasn't started, punctuation the transcriber added using later audio.

The two dials you control

Which moments count as "about to raise my hand"

Below is one real-shaped hand raise at 0 s and the 2-second moments before and after it. H is how far ahead the model looks. g is your rule that it must call it at least g seconds early, so it can't just see the hand going up.

"Will my hand go up in the next H seconds?" Headline: 15 s.
Must predict at least g seconds before the hand. Swept at 1, 2, 3 and 5 s.
positive: hand goes up in (t + g, t + H] negative: no hand in that window not used: too close to the raise, or hand already up

Step 3, up close

Train, validation and test: split by whole classes

Each square is one class. The test 20% is locked away by a hash of the class id and scored once, at the end. The rest rotate: in each round the model trains on four groups and is checked on the fifth (validation). After five rounds every class has been validated exactly once. Click a round.

train validation (this round) test (locked)

Illustrative: 694 squares stand for the recorded classes. The real split is computed after cleaning, on the classes that pass it, with the same hash rule.
