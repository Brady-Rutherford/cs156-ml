A pre-registered plan for a machine-learning pipeline

Can a model hear my cue before my hand goes up?

Every two seconds of a Minerva class, On Cue asks whether I will raise my hand in the next 15 seconds, using only what I could have heard, seen in Forum, or written before class up to that second. The labels come from the raised-hand icon in the class recording: no faces, no tone of voice, no other students' hands. The real question is whether what is said, and how it matches my pre-class work, tells the model more than when it is said.

The causal contract, at one second

What the model is allowed to know at time t

Three kinds of input, each cut at t: the last 60 seconds of speech, the state of the class so far, and what existed before class. A word counts only once it has ended plus a 0.25 s margin; a word still being spoken shows that someone is talking, not what they said. The two dials

Horizon H and guard g: how far ahead, and how early it must commit

The guard is my dial: the model must fire at least g seconds before the icon, swept over 1, 2, 3 and 5 s (headline 2 s). The horizon sets how far ahead a bid still counts. Every 2-second point in this synthetic 88-minute class is positive, negative, undefined (guard, votes, glitches, unobserved spans), or not at risk at all (breakouts, while I talk, while my hand is up, 10 s after it comes down). The model menu

Baselines first, then learners that must beat them

No published study forecasts a student's hand raise from classroom talk, so simple rules are the yardstick and the honest target is "a few times the base rate". The ranking follows the model-options research: a hazard-style logistic regression and gradient boosting do the heavy lifting; text enters as cues, embeddings and stacked scores rather than end-to-end networks.

Ways this could fool us

Ten leakage traps, and the defence built in against each

Words not yet said

A 60-second window that ends at t can include a word that ends a moment later.

Each word is usable only from end + 0.25 s; two code paths must agree.

check · truncation + sentinel tests, blocking

Punctuation from the future

Batch ASR places a "?" using the pause that follows it: the very pause that opens the floor.

Headline text is lowercase with no punctuation; F-punct measures what it would add.

check · A-stream re-transcribes [t − 180, t]

"[NAME], go ahead"

The professor reacts to my hand a second or two before I speak.

Call-on phrases and name tokens are deleted; points with my hand up or just lowered are not at risk.

check · CALLON rate ratio must include 1

The key press in the transcript

Scribe can write my H presses into the transcript as events.

Event tables come only from raw class-events; the live log is QA-only.

check · loader allowlist test

A calibration that says zero

Scribe's whole-class fallback reports a residual of 0.0 and status "ok".

QA needs a windowed fit with ≥ 6 windows and residual p95 ≤ 1.5 s.

check · P-clock curve must peak at the border lag

Which class, not which moment

"He talks a lot in this course" scores well on pooled AP.

No class ids or dates; the primary statistic is within-class AP_w.

check · TG vs TG_local shifts

Votes that look like bids

"Raise your hand if…" makes every hand go up at once: trivially predictable.

Votes are typed by other tiles rising and by the vote lexicon, and their windows are undefined.

check · vote sensitivities, drop-column AP

Neighbouring points

Points 2 s apart overlap in every window they read.

Whole classes are held out; vocabularies count classes, not rows.

check · the random-split tax, reported as a number

Workbooks edited after class

A PCW answer revised during class is future information.

Answers come only from docs verified as of class start; status is QA, never a feature.

check · funnel by PCW status

My history from the test fold

"My bid rate in this course" computed from all classes includes the ones being scored.

Built from earlier classes in the training fold only.

check · fold-aware unit test

The definitions that matter

Labels, availability, and a metric built for the question

Label at decision point t

Positives need observation only up to their onset; negatives need the whole window. A discrete-time hazard, scored on a 2-second grid.

When a word becomes usable

εw = 0.25 s. The speaker role comes from the Forum voice event e(w) that overlaps the word, available εF after it starts (εF per class, from calibration residuals).

Firing rule

An alarm then stays quiet for H − g = 13 s. 𝒢 keeps every point not removed by a causal rule.

False alarms per at-risk minute

Headline budgets 0.1 and 0.5 per minute (6 and 30 per hour); thresholds matched on inner folds, never on test.

Event recall

A bid counts as caught if some firing lands between H and g seconds before it. Reported with time in warning and median lead.

Chance recall

Any firing process at rate λf that ignores the onsets catches this share of bids. Always-on fires every 14 s and scores ≈ chance.

EventLift

Primary chance level: the same firing rule on the model's own predictions, shifted in time, at the false-alarm rate it actually reached.

Within-class AP (primary)

Kc is the number of bids in class c. Which classes I talk in cannot inflate it.

The moment, not the phase

APlocal shifts predictions by ≥ 30 s inside 3-minute same-segment blocks: it keeps the class, segment and minute, and breaks only second-scale timing.

Brier skill score

At π ≈ 2% the constant forecaster already scores a Brier of about 0.02, so raw Brier flatters every model.

Guard and horizon in one number

Each bid yields this many positive grid points, so π, and every AP read against it, moves with both dials.

Unspent preparation

aj are chunks of my PCW answers, x the recency-pooled embedding of the room, M≤t what I have already said. Similarities are z-scored against earlier classes' chunks. High U: the room has reached something I prepared and have not said yet.
